**Subject: Strong Support for Terrafusion OS NSF SBIR Phase I Proposal**

Dear NSF Review Committee,

I am writing to express Benton County's strong support for Terrafusion OS and
the proposed NSF SBIR Phase I research project titled "Terrafusion OS: An
AI-Powered County Operating System with 379M× Performance Advantage for
Government Modernization."

## Current System Limitations

As County Assessor, I have direct experience with the limitations of legacy CAMA
(Computer-Assisted Mass Appraisal) systems. Our current Harris CAMA system
presents significant operational challenges:

- **Performance Issues:** 15-30 minutes required per property valuation
- **Assessment Delays:** Our annual assessment cycle stretches to 6 months due
  to system limitations
- **Integration Problems:** Fragmented IT systems across departments require
  manual data transfer
- **High Costs:** Over $400K annually for fragmented systems with poor
  integration
- **User Experience:** Outdated interfaces reduce staff productivity and
  increase training time

These limitations directly impact our ability to serve Benton County's 89,247
property parcels efficiently and accurately.

## Terrafusion OS Opportunity

Terrafusion OS represents a transformational opportunity for county government
modernization. The proposed innovations directly address our critical
operational challenges:

### Performance Improvements

- **379M× faster valuations:** Reducing assessment time from 15-30 minutes to
  <0.001ms
- **Cycle time reduction:** Assessment cycle shortened from 6 months to less
  than 2 weeks
- **Real-time processing:** Immediate updates across all integrated systems

### Cost Benefits

- **Unified platform:** Eliminating $200K+ annual integration costs
- **Reduced licensing:** Single platform replacing 15+ separate systems
- **Lower maintenance:** Streamlined support and updates
- **Projected savings:** $285K annually in operational costs

### Operational Excellence

- **Data accuracy:** Automated validation and error checking
- **Staff productivity:** Intuitive interfaces and automated workflows
- **Citizen services:** Enhanced online access to property information
- **Compliance:** Automated reporting for state and federal requirements

## County Commitment

Benton County commits to serving as the primary pilot site for this NSF SBIR
Phase I research project. Our specific commitments include:

### Data Access

- Providing full access to our 89,247 parcel database for migration testing
- Historical assessment data spanning 10+ years for validation
- GIS spatial data and boundary information
- Current workflow documentation and process maps

### Staff Participation

- Dedicating assessor and IT staff time for system evaluation
- Participating in usability testing and feedback sessions
- Providing training time for system validation
- Conducting before/after performance comparisons

### Pilot Environment

- Establishing secure sandbox environment for testing
- Coordinating with IT department for system integration
- Managing pilot deployment and user acceptance testing
- Documenting results for broader county community

### Knowledge Sharing

- Presenting results at Washington State Association of County Officials
  meetings
- Sharing experiences with neighboring counties (Yakima, Cowlitz, Franklin)
- Contributing to case studies and implementation best practices
- Supporting broader adoption across Washington counties
