### Standard Operating Procedure: The Benton Method Equity Control & Calibration Loop

#### 1.0 Purpose, Scope, and Applicability

##### 1.1 Introduction: The Strategic Importance of Vertical Equity Governance

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) is a critical governance document for the valuation office. Maintaining vertical equity in property assessment is no longer a mere technical requirement; it is a core component of public trust, operational credibility, and legal defensibility. This procedure establishes a systematic, repeatable, and evidence-based approach to the management of our valuation models, ensuring that our processes for diagnosing and correcting inequity are transparent, targeted, and effective.

##### 1.2 Purpose

The primary purposes of this SOP are:

* To provide a formal, repeatable procedure for diagnosing and correcting vertical inequity in property valuations.  
* To standardize the use of the Benton Method's diagnostic stack: using the PRD as a  **signal** , the PRB as a formal  **test** , and decile/segment analysis as the  **explanation**  for any identified bias.  
* To establish a clear protocol for documenting all model calibrations via a standard Calibration Memo, creating an auditable record of changes.  
* To ensure all valuation changes are fully defensible for appeals, audits, and legislative scrutiny.

##### 1.3 Scope

This procedure applies to the entire equity control lifecycle. This scope includes the initial triggering of a model review based on statistical signals, the subsequent diagnostic analysis to identify root causes of inequity, the implementation of corrective model tuning, and the final verification and documentation of all changes.

##### 1.4 Applicability

This SOP is mandatory for all personnel involved in the development, management, and review of valuation models and ratio studies. This includes, but is not limited to, Assessors, Appraisal Supervisors, and Valuation Analysts. The procedures outlined herein are guided by the foundational principles of the Benton Method, which are designed to produce valuations that are inherently fair and defensible.

#### 2.0 Foundational Principles of the Benton Method

##### 2.1 Introduction: Establishing a Defensible Valuation Architecture

The procedures detailed in this document are based on the core architectural principles of the Benton Method. These principles are not arbitrary rules but are designed to create a valuation system that is more equitable, transparent, and easier to defend by systematically addressing common sources of valuation bias.

##### 2.2 Principle 1: Mitigating Inequity with Proportional Adjustments

A primary failure mode of traditional valuation models is the use of flat-dollar adjustments for property features, which do not scale across different value tiers. This practice can introduce predictable regressivity. For example, applying a flat  **\+$8,000**  adjustment for a covered patio disproportionately impacts a lower-value property compared to a higher-value one.The Benton Method solves this by mandating the use of  **%-of-base schedules** . A covered patio valued at  **\+3% of the base improvement value**  ensures the adjustment scales proportionally and equitably across all value tiers, adhering to the principle:  **"Scale the Feature, Not the Narrative."**

##### 2.3 Principle 2: Separating Objective and Subjective Components

Subjective elements like property quality and condition, if not managed systematically, can become uncontrolled sources of valuation bias. This method mandates the separation of objective, measurable property features from subjective assessments. By using structured tools like quality and condition calculators, we capture these elements in a systematic and repeatable manner. If diagnostic analysis (Section 3.4) indicates that inconsistent quality or condition grades are a driver of inequity, the review and calibration of these calculators becomes the designated corrective action.

##### 2.4 Principle 3: The Market Calibration Loop

The engine of the Benton Method is a continuous, iterative market calibration loop. This is not a one-time setup but an ongoing process of model refinement based on real-world market evidence. The loop consists of four distinct steps performed in sequence:**Sales Evidence → Diagnostics → Schedule Tuning → Verification**These principles guide the specific diagnostic and corrective actions that form the core of our equity control procedures.

#### 3.0 Procedure: The Equity Control Loop \- Diagnostic Phase

##### 3.1 Introduction: From Signal to Root Cause

The purpose of the diagnostic phase is to conduct a structured investigation that moves from a high-level signal of potential inequity to a precise identification of its root cause. This systematic process prevents analysts from "chasing" metrics or making untargeted adjustments, ensuring that corrective actions are precise and effective. Adherence to this diagnostic sequence is mandatory to ensure all subsequent corrective actions are evidence-based and legally defensible.

##### 3.2 Step 1: Identify Initial Signals with the Price-Related Differential (PRD)

The Price-Related Differential (PRD) serves as the initial  **"signal"**  of potential vertical equity bias. A PRD that falls outside of the established acceptable range does not, by itself, confirm inequity. Rather, it serves as a formal trigger, mandating that the analyst proceed to the next step in the diagnostic process for formal testing.

##### 3.3 Step 2: Conduct Confirmatory Testing with the Price-Related Bias (PRB)

The Price-Related Bias (PRB) is the formal  **"test"**  for vertical equity bias. The analyst must calculate the PRB and interpret its slope. A positive slope confirms progressive bias, while a negative slope confirms regressive bias. This step moves beyond the initial signal to formally confirm the presence and direction of the inequity.

##### 3.4 Step 3: Isolate Drivers with Decile and Segment Analysis

This is the most critical step of the diagnosis, providing the  **"explanation"**  for the bias confirmed by the PRB. The analyst must review ratio study outputs that are broken down by value deciles and other relevant market segments (e.g., age cohort, size cohort, geographic area). By examining performance across these segments, the analyst must pinpoint which specific part of the model schedule or which property population is the primary driver of the inequity. Once the driver is isolated, the analyst shall proceed to the corrective action phase.

#### 4.0 Procedure: The Equity Control Loop \- Corrective Action Phase

##### 4.1 Introduction: Applying the Lowest-Collateral Fix

The philosophy behind the corrective action phase is to resolve the identified inequity while minimizing unintended consequences to the rest of the valuation model. This is achieved by using the "Action Ladder" and adhering to the principle of selecting the  **"lowest-collateral fix first."**  This approach preserves the stability of the overall model by ensuring that the intervention is as targeted as possible.

##### 4.2 Step 1: Select the Corrective Action Using the Action Ladder

Based on the diagnostic findings from Section 3.0, the analyst must select an action from the following ladder, beginning with the lowest-collateral option supported by the diagnostic evidence:

1. **(Preferred) Schedule Scaling:**  As the lowest-collateral fix, adjustments to %-of-base schedules for specific property features (e.g., basements, patios, decks) are the first action to be considered if diagnostic analysis points to a specific feature as problematic.  
2. **Market Segmentation:**  If schedule scaling is insufficient or diagnostics show inequity is driven by distinct market behaviors, the analyst may apply different valuation models or schedules to these segments (e.g., creating separate schedules for historic homes versus new construction).  
3. **Depreciation Curve Adjustment:**  This is a high-collateral action reserved for cases where decile and segment analysis clearly identifies property age as the primary and systemic driver of inequity across the entire model.

##### 4.3 Step 2: Implement and Verify the Model Calibration

The analyst is required to implement the chosen corrective action in the CAMA system. Following implementation, verification is mandatory. The analyst must re-run the ratio studies on the newly adjusted model to confirm that the change has corrected the identified inequity and has not negatively impacted the overall assessment level or uniformity. This verification step formally closes the calibration loop, confirming that the model tuning was both effective and safe. The process then returns to a state of monitoring until a new trigger is identified.

#### 5.0 Procedure: Governance and Documentation Protocol

##### 5.1 Introduction: Ensuring Transparency and Defensibility

Rigorous governance and documentation are essential for maintaining the credibility of our valuation models. This protocol ensures that we can prove that all model changes are evidence-based, data-driven calibrations and not arbitrary adjustments made to "tune for optics." This documentation is our primary tool for defending our valuations.

##### 5.2 Change Triggers and Version Control

A formal model review is triggered whenever key performance metrics, such as the PRD or PRB, fall outside of established thresholds or when ongoing market analysis indicates a need for recalibration.A strict version control protocol is mandatory for all valuation schedules. Any change to a schedule, no matter how minor, must result in a new version number (e.g., schedule\_v1.1 is superseded by schedule\_v1.2) and an associated effective date. This creates a clear, auditable history of the model's evolution.

##### 5.3 The Calibration Memo Standard

Any change to the valuation model is incomplete until a formal Calibration Memo has been drafted, reviewed, and signed in accordance with this protocol. The analyst responsible for the change must draft a memo that includes all of the following sections:

* **Purpose/Trigger:**  A clear statement of the metric, market evidence, or event that prompted this review.  
* **Data Used:**  A description of the sales window, data cleaning rules, and any specific data exclusions applied during the analysis.  
* **Diagnostics Summary:**  A summary of the PRD, PRB, and decile/segment analysis that identified the problem and guided the corrective action.  
* **Change Made:**  A precise description of the change implemented, including the old and new schedule version numbers and the effective date. This versioning creates a precise, auditable history of the model's calibration, which is essential for defending changes over time.  
* **Expected Impact:**  A statement on the anticipated effect of the change on vertical equity metrics and the overall assessment level.  
* **Verification Plan:**  The results of the post-change ratio study that confirm the efficacy of the change, along with the date scheduled for the next routine review of the affected model component.  
* **Sign-off/Review Chain:**  A section for approval signatures from the analyst, the supervising appraiser, and the Chief Appraiser/Assessor.The completed and signed memo serves as the official record and primary defensibility document for the model change.
