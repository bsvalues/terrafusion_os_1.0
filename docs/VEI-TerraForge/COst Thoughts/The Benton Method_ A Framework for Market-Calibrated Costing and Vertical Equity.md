### The Benton Method: A Framework for Market-Calibrated Costing and Vertical Equity

#### Executive Summary

This document provides a comprehensive overview of the Benton Method, a market-calibrated cost approach designed to address and correct vertical inequity in property valuation. The method identifies a primary cause of valuation regressivity: the use of flat-dollar adjustments for property features, which do not scale across different value tiers and disproportionately impact lower-value properties.The core solution proposed by the Benton Method is the implementation of  **"%-of-base schedules,"**  which ensure that feature adjustments are proportional to a property's base value, thus preserving equity. This approach is integrated within a systematic framework that includes:

* An  **Equity Control Loop**  for ongoing monitoring and correction, utilizing a diagnostic stack of metrics (PRD, PRB, and decile/segment analysis) to identify the specific drivers of inequity.  
* A  **Market Calibration Loop**  that mandates tuning valuation schedules against real sales evidence and verifying the results.  
* A strong emphasis on  **Governance and Defensibility** , requiring rigorous documentation through version control, change logs, and formal calibration memos to ensure transparency and withstand scrutiny from appeals boards and legislative bodies.The method provides a technical, defensible process for improving valuation accuracy and fairness, while also offering clear scripts for communicating these complex topics to non-technical stakeholders.

#### 1\. The Central Problem: Vertical Inequity and Its Primary Cause

The Benton Method frames vertical equity not merely as a technical metric but as a critical issue of governance and public credibility. It posits that valuation systems can be compliant on overall assessment levels (e.g., median ratio) while simultaneously failing on vertical equity, leading to regressive outcomes.

##### The Predictable Failure Mode: Flat-Dollar Adjustments

The primary mechanism identified as a source of vertical inequity is the common practice of using flat-dollar (or lump-sum) adjustments for property features.

* **The Scale Effect:**  A fixed-value adjustment, such as \+$8,000 for a covered patio, represents a much larger percentage of a low-tier property's base value (e.g., $250,000) than a high-tier property's base value (e.g., $650,000).  
* **"Feature Share" Distortion:**  This "scale effect" distorts the "feature share" across value tiers, creating a predictable bias that tends to overvalue improvements on lower-cost homes and contribute to regressivity.  
* **Common Examples:**  This issue is prevalent in adjustments for features like patios, decks, finished basements, shops, and outbuildings.

#### 2\. The Core Solution: The Benton Method's Architecture

To counter the failure of flat-dollar adjustments, the Benton Method introduces a new valuation architecture centered on proportionality and market evidence. The guiding principle is to "Scale the Feature, Not the Narrative."

##### Key Components of the Method

* **%-of-Base Schedules:**  The foundational element is replacing lump-sum additions with schedules that calculate adjustments as a percentage of the base improvement value. For example, a covered patio might add 3% to the base value, ensuring the adjustment scales equitably across all price tiers.  
* **Separation of Subjective and Objective Factors:**  The method advocates for using systematic "quality and condition calculators" to handle subjective assessments. This isolates subjective judgments from the core, objective, market-calibrated schedules.  
* **Disciplined Feature Tracking:**  A core discipline of the method is to "track what drives value," promoting a minimalist and defensible approach to which features are included in the valuation model.

#### 3\. The Equity Control Loop: A Diagnostic and Corrective Framework

The Benton Method is not a one-time fix but a continuous improvement process governed by an "Equity Control Loop." This loop consists of market calibration and a precise diagnostic sequence.

##### The Market Calibration Loop

This process ensures that valuation schedules remain aligned with local market behavior through a four-step cycle:

1. **Sales Evidence:**  Analyze a clean and relevant set of sales data.  
2. **Diagnostics:**  Run the diagnostic stack to identify any potential bias.  
3. **Schedule Tuning:**  Adjust the %-of-base schedules based on the diagnostic findings.  
4. **Verification:**  Verify that the changes have had the intended effect on equity and level.

##### The Diagnostic Stack

The method prescribes a specific sequence for interpreting statistical metrics to avoid "chasing" numbers and to pinpoint the root cause of inequity.

* **PRD (Price-Related Differential):**  Used as an initial  **signal**  of potential bias.  
* **PRB (Price-Related Bias):**  Used as a definitive  **test**  to confirm the presence and direction (regressive or progressive) of bias.  
* **Deciles & Segments:**  Used as the  **explanation**  to locate the driver of the inequity, breaking down data by value tiers, property size, age, or other relevant cohorts.

##### The Action Ladder

Once a driver of inequity is identified, the method calls for selecting the  **lowest-collateral corrective action**  first. This means choosing the most targeted fix with the fewest unintended consequences, such as adjusting schedule scaling before resorting to broader actions like creating new market segments or altering depreciation curves.

#### 4\. Governance and Defensibility: Ensuring Credibility

A central theme of the method is that a robust governance framework is essential for proving that valuation changes are methodical and evidence-based, not arbitrary or "tuned for optics."

##### Governance Components

* **Triggers:**  Establishing clear thresholds (e.g., a specific PRB value or decile pattern) that trigger a formal review of valuation schedules.  
* **Version Control:**  Systematically versioning schedules to track changes over time.  
* **Change Log:**  Maintaining a detailed log of all modifications.  
* **Calibration Memo:**  A formal, 1-2 page document that serves as the official record for any schedule change.

##### The Calibration Memo

This document is a key tool for defensibility and should include:

* **Purpose/Trigger:**  The metric or evidence that prompted the review.  
* **Data Used:**  The sales window and data cleaning rules.  
* **Diagnostics Summary:**  Key findings from the PRD/PRB/decile analysis.  
* **Change Made:**  The specific schedule version change and its effective date.  
* **Expected Impact:**  The anticipated effect on equity and level.  
* **Verification Plan:**  The timeline and thresholds for the next review.  
* **Sign-Off:**  An approval chain for accountability.

#### 6\. Training and Implementation Overview

The principles of the Benton Method are delivered through structured presenting courses designed for the International Association of Assessing Officers (IAAO), available in 60-minute and 90-minute formats.

* **Target Audience:**  Assessors, deputies, appraisal supervisors, analysts, modelers, ratio study staff, CAMA administrators, and GIS leads.  
* **Prerequisites:**  Basic comfort with median ratio, COD, and PRD definitions.
