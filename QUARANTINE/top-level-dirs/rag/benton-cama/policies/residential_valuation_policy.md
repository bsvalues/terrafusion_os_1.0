# Residential Valuation Policy

## Benton County Residential Property Assessment Policy

**Effective Date**: January 1, 2024  
**Last Updated**: December 2024  
**Authority**: RCW 84.40.030, WAC 458-07

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy establishes the procedures and standards for valuing residential properties in Benton County, Washington. All residential assessments shall reflect fair market value as of January 1 of the assessment year.

## 2. Property Classification Criteria

### 2.1 Single-Family Residential (SFR)
Properties meeting the following criteria:
- One dwelling unit on a single parcel
- Primarily residential use (≥80% of value)
- Structures include: site-built homes, modular homes, manufactured homes on permanent foundations

### 2.2 Multi-Family Residential
- 2-4 unit residential properties
- Properties with 5+ units classified as commercial/income

### 2.3 Condominiums
- Individual units within a condominium development
- Common area interest included in unit assessment

## 3. Valuation Methodology

### 3.1 Primary Approach: Sales Comparison
The sales comparison approach is the primary method for residential valuation:

1. **Comparable Selection Criteria**:
   - Sale date within 12 months (extended to 24 months if insufficient data)
   - Similar property characteristics
   - Arms-length transactions only
   - Same or similar neighborhood/market area

2. **Adjustment Factors**:
   - **Time Adjustment**: Market trend analysis applied monthly
   - **Location Adjustment**: Based on neighborhood analysis
   - **Physical Adjustments**: 
     - Living area: $85-125/sq ft (varies by quality grade)
     - Garage: $35-50/sq ft
     - Basement: $25-40/sq ft (finished), $15-25/sq ft (unfinished)
     - Lot size: $0.50-3.00/sq ft (varies by location)
     - Age: 0.5-1.5% per year (varies by condition)

3. **Quality Grade Scale** (Benton County):
   | Grade | Description | Base $/SF |
   |-------|-------------|-----------|
   | A | Luxury/Custom | $200+ |
   | B+ | High Quality | $160-200 |
   | B | Good Quality | $130-160 |
   | C+ | Above Average | $110-130 |
   | C | Average | $90-110 |
   | D | Fair/Below Average | $70-90 |
   | E | Poor | $50-70 |

### 3.2 Cost Approach (Secondary)
Used when:
- Insufficient comparable sales
- New construction
- Unique/custom properties

Cost approach formula:
```
Replacement Cost New
- Physical Depreciation
- Functional Obsolescence
- External Obsolescence
+ Land Value
= Indicated Value
```

## 4. Data Collection Standards

### 4.1 Required Property Characteristics
- Gross living area (GLA) - above grade
- Basement area (total and finished)
- Garage/carport area
- Year built / Effective age
- Number of bedrooms and bathrooms
- Quality grade and condition rating
- Heating/cooling systems
- Special features (pools, outbuildings, etc.)

### 4.2 Inspection Cycle
- Full property reinspection: Every 6 years
- Permit-triggered inspections: Within 6 months of completion
- Sales-triggered reviews: Within 90 days

## 5. Special Situations

### 5.1 New Construction
- Values based on cost approach during construction
- Market comparison applied upon completion
- Partial assessments for incomplete structures

### 5.2 Manufactured Homes
- Must be on permanent foundation for real property classification
- Otherwise assessed as personal property
- Age-life depreciation tables applied

### 5.3 Senior/Disabled Exemptions
- Per RCW 84.36.379-389
- Income-based property tax relief
- Separate application process

## 6. Quality Control

### 6.1 Ratio Study Requirements
- **Median Ratio Target**: 95-100% of market value
- **Coefficient of Dispersion (COD)**: ≤15 for residential
- **Price-Related Differential (PRD)**: 0.98-1.03

### 6.2 Appeals Process
1. Informal review with assessor staff
2. Board of Equalization (BOE) appeal
3. State Board of Tax Appeals (BTA)
4. Superior Court

## 7. Effective Dates

- **Assessment Date**: January 1
- **Valuation Date**: January 1
- **Physical Condition Date**: January 1
