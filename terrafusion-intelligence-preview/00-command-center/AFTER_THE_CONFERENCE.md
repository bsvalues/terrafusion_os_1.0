# After the Conference

Written before the conference. Not after.

Because if you write success criteria after you see the results, you'll move the goalposts.

---

## What Would Make This Conference a Success?

### Tier 1 — The product communicated

- [ ] At least 3 people describe Atlas as "decisions" or "understanding" — not "reports" or "dashboards"
- [ ] At least 2 people describe Academy as "learning" or "capability" — not "training" or "documentation"
- [ ] At least 1 person uses the phrase "what to do next" or equivalent when describing TerraFusion
- [ ] Nobody says "I don't get it"

### Tier 2 — The product resonated

- [ ] 5+ meaningful follow-up conversations (not polite nods — real questions about their county)
- [ ] 3+ counties wanting a deeper demo with their own data
- [ ] 2+ people asking about Academy access for their staff
- [ ] 1+ person saying "this solves a real problem we have"

### Tier 3 — The doctrine held

- [ ] The most-asked question is about outcomes, not technology
- [ ] Nobody asks "what AI model does this use?" as their first question
- [ ] The conference story (3-min version) was delivered without rambling
- [ ] The demo ran without a fallback

---

## What to Watch For

### Signals that the product is working

- People lean in during Signals section
- People ask "can I see this for my county?"
- People take photos of the screen
- People ask about Academy topics relevant to their job
- The word "decisions" appears in their questions

### Signals that something needs work

- People ask "but where's the map?" as their first reaction (expected — have the answer ready)
- People glaze over during Atlas Insight (it may still be too long)
- People don't click Academy after seeing Atlas (the bridge may need work)
- People ask about pricing before asking about capability (positioning didn't land)

---

## What NOT to Do at the Conference

- Do not demo TerraFusion OS unless someone asks
- Do not mention agents, architecture, RAG, MCP, or swarm
- Do not say "AI-powered" — say "decision support"
- Do not apologize for demo data — lead with it ("This is Benton County data. The structure is production-ready.")
- Do not promise features that don't exist
- Do not take feature requests during the demo — write them down, nod, move on

---

## What to Do Immediately After

1. Write down every question people asked — exact words, not paraphrases
2. Write down what people remembered (ask them)
3. Write down what nobody asked about — that's your backlog signal
4. Compare against this document
5. Do not start building for 48 hours

That last one matters. The temptation after a successful demo is to immediately start adding. Resist it. Let the feedback settle. Then decide.

---

## The Real Success Metric

Not downloads. Not signups. Not applause.

One sentence:

**Did people leave understanding what TerraFusion does, without anyone having to explain it to them?**

If yes: the doctrine is real.

If no: the doctrine needs work.

Either answer is valuable. Only one is available if you wrote the criteria first.
