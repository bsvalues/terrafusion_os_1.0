# Presentation Strategy

> Nobody remembers how it looks. Everybody remembers what it says.

---

## 1. The Opening (First 30 Seconds)

Three versions. Each under 15 seconds. No technology words.

**Version A — The Drowning Frame**

"Every office in this room has more data than ever and less time to make sense of it. What if your next property review started with the one sentence that actually matters?"

**Version B — The Three-Screen Frame**

"You pull up PACS. You open GIS. You check your sales spreadsheet. Twenty minutes later, you've built the picture yourself. What if the picture was already there when you sat down?"

**Version C — The Monday Morning Frame**

"It's Monday morning. You have 87 appeals, a senior exemption backlog worth a quarter million dollars, and a commissioner meeting Thursday. Where do you start? That's the question we built this to answer."

## 3. The Ten Pivots

When someone pulls toward technology, pivot to outcomes. Every pivot follows the same structure: acknowledge, bridge, redirect to a decision or a person.

**1. "What AI model does this use?"**

"The model matters less than the output. What matters is whether this sentence — 'Hold position, watch the commercial development' — is something your assessor would actually agree with. So far, every senior assessor we've tested it with has. Would you agree with it?"

**2. "How does the data pipeline work?"**

"It starts with the same data you already have — PACS, sales, permits. The difference is what comes out the other end. Instead of a spreadsheet, you get a decision. Want me to show you what that looks like for a property you'd recognize?"

**3. "What's the architecture?"**

"I could walk you through the architecture, but honestly, what our testers cared about was whether it helped them make faster decisions. One of our field appraisers said, 'PACS gives me data. This gives me decisions.' That's the architecture that matters. Want to see what he saw?"

**4. "Is this machine learning?"**

"There's some of that under the hood, but the real question is: does it get the assessment right? We tested it against a 15-year senior assessor and a field appraiser with 8 years. Both validated the output. The confidence matters more than the method."

**5. "How many agents?"**

"The number isn't what matters — what matters is what shows up on your screen. One sentence that tells you whether to hold, adjust, or investigate further. That's it. Would you like to see a property?"

**6. "Can you explain the RAG implementation?"**

"I'd rather show you what it produces. Ask it any assessment question — BOE prep, senior exemptions, commercial depreciation — and see if the answer is something you'd trust. That's a better test than me explaining the internals."

**7. "What database does this run on?"**

"Whatever your county already uses. The value isn't in the database — it's in the fact that your staff can sit down Monday morning and know exactly where to start. One of our testers said, 'Here's what to do Monday morning.' That's the goal."

**8. "How does it handle data security / compliance?"**

"Every county's data stays isolated. Nobody sees another county's information. We're building for government standards from day one — not retrofitting compliance after the fact. Happy to go deeper on that in a separate conversation if you need the specifics."

**9. "Is this cloud or on-prem?"**

"We can do either, depending on what your county requires. But the question I'd ask first is whether the output is useful. If the decisions it surfaces are wrong, it doesn't matter where it runs. Can I show you a property and you tell me if the assessment guidance is sound?"

**10. "What makes this different from [competitor]?"**

"I won't knock anyone else's product. What I'll show you is this: pull up any property, and the first thing you see is one sentence telling you what matters and what to do about it. If other tools do that, great. If not, that's the difference. Want to try one?"

### Script Rules (for the presenter)

- If you catch yourself about to say "AI," say "the analysis" instead.
- If you catch yourself about to say "agent," say "the review" instead.
- If you catch yourself about to say "platform" or "engine," say "this" instead.
- If you catch yourself about to say "algorithm," say "the method" instead.
- If you catch yourself about to say "architecture," stop talking and point at the screen.
- If someone asks a technical question mid-demo, say "Great question — let me finish this property and then I'll come back to that." Then pivot (Section 3).

## 6. The Closing (Last 30 Seconds)

Three versions. Each references what users actually remembered. Each ends with a question.

**Version A — The Decision Close**

"Everything you just saw comes down to one idea: your time is too valuable to spend building the picture. The picture should be there when you sit down. We're working with Benton County to make that real. What's the first thing you'd want to see for your county?"

**Version B — The Monday Morning Close**

"When we tested this, five different people in a county office — from a 15-year assessor to a hire with 18 months — all said the same thing: 'I know what to do Monday morning.' That's what we're building. What does your Monday morning look like?"

**Version C — The Capability Close**

"One of our newer staff looked at this and said, 'I don't feel like an expert. But I feel like I could become one.' That sentence is why we built this. If your office has people at different experience levels trying to do consistent work — what would it mean if they all started with the same understanding?"
